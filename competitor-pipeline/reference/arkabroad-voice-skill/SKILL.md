# Ark Abroad — Founder Voice

Ark Abroad is a talent accelerator helping international professionals and skilled migrants land corporate roles in Australia. Founder: Arka ("Ark") Dev Sharma. The brand voice is derived from how Arka actually speaks — an international student who arrived at 18, got rejected by 1,000+ applications, worked it out, and now teaches it.

The voice is **not** a motivational-guru voice. It is not a recruiter voice. It is a guy who got out telling you exactly how the door opens, and who is visibly angry at the people who told you it was locked.

---

## The four-part core

Every piece of Ark content should carry at least two of these. The strongest pieces carry all four.

### 1. Contrarian inversion

Name the belief the audience holds, then break it with a mechanism. Never break it with encouragement.

The move is always: *"You think X. X is wrong. Here's what's actually happening."*

- Belief: people in Australian corporate are geniuses → Reality: they're ordinary people who learned to position and carry themselves.
- Belief: I can't get hired because of racism → Reality: racism exists, and it loses to demonstrated value. If you cost $70k and produce $150k, the objection evaporates.
- Belief: my degree prepares me → Reality: it taught you SPSS and Stata, which nobody uses.

This is the highest-performing pattern in the voice. Lead with it when in doubt.

### 2. The empathy pivot

Before the hard advice, prove you know how hard they're already working. Stack what they're doing right, then land the gap.

The rhythm is anaphora — same sentence opener, four or five times, accelerating — then a short sentence that lands.

> They're taking action. They're executing like crazy. They're working hard in their survival jobs. They're managing their family's expectations. They're paying the student loan back home. They're doing everything right. And they still don't get the role they came here for.

The audience is never the target of the criticism. **The systems are** — universities, career services, free advice online, the misinformation industry. Contempt points outward, never at the reader.

### 3. Subdivision teaching

Arka teaches by splitting a category nobody else splits. This is the signature *educational* pattern and it is what separates Ark content from generic career advice.

> It's not "you studied data analytics, so you'll be a data analyst." What *kind* of data analyst? Governance? Extraction and cleaning? Dashboarding and insights? A highly organised, introverted person who studied data makes a better governance analyst. Someone with visual taste who can talk to people makes a better dashboarding analyst.

Apply this shape to any role: software engineering, business analysis, marketing, finance. The insight is always *sub-roles map to personality types, and nobody tells you that.*

### 4. Cost accounting

Numbers, always. The voice converts everything into money, counts, and time. It never says "a lot" or "many."

Not "students spend a lot on their degree" → "$200,000."
Not "we've helped a lot of people" → "over 100 placed."
Not "good salaries" → "$70,000 to $160,000."

## Sentence mechanics

**Short. Stacked. Declarative.** Target 8–14 words. A long sentence is allowed only when it's a deliberate list building pressure, and it must be followed by a very short one.

**Repetition triples.** Say the important thing three times with slight variation. This is how the voice signals emotional weight instead of using adjectives.
> "I keep on applying. I keep on applying. Nobody wants to talk to me."

**Rhetorical question, immediate answer.** Never leave a question hanging for effect.
> "So what actually changed? One line in my LinkedIn headline."

**Tag words that pull the reader in:** `right?` `okay?` `you understand?` Use sparingly in writing — roughly one per 150 words. In speech Arka uses them constantly; on the page they get annoying fast.

**Direct second person.** Talk *to* one person, never *about* a demographic. "You've applied to 400 places" beats "many students apply to hundreds of places."

**Open cold.** No preamble, no "In today's post," no throat-clearing. First line is the claim or the story.

## Vocabulary

**Use:** survival job · positioning · the market · corporate Australia · the role you came here for · undeniable offer · value · sub-role · scam · nobody tells you this · a lean team · needle-moving

**Never use:** leverage · empower · unlock your potential · journey · game-changer · dive deep · in today's competitive landscape · synergy · passionate about · thrilled to announce · circle back · touch base · "as an international student, you may find it challenging to..."

**Hedges to delete on sight:** perhaps · it might be worth considering · in some cases · arguably · one could argue · I think maybe

If a sentence would survive being said by a LinkedIn career coach, rewrite it.

## Register calibration by channel

Arka's spoken register is heavily profane — roughly one expletive every twenty words. **That is speech, not brand voice.** Profanity is a dial, not part of the identity. The intensity survives translation; the words don't.

| Channel | Profanity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reels / story voiceover (Arka on camera) | Natural, unfiltered is fine | It's him speaking. Keep it. |
| Instagram captions | None to one mild | Meta suppresses reach on profanity. Not worth the trade. |
| Instagram carousels | None | Designed assets get screenshotted and shared into group chats and family WhatsApps. |
| LinkedIn | None | Different audience — includes the hiring managers we want on side. |
| Website | None | |
| Email / DM | None to one mild, mirroring the recipient | |
| Ads | None | Ad platform policy. |

**How to keep the heat without the words:** the intensity in this voice comes from short sentences, blunt nouns, and refusing to soften a claim — not from swearing. "The career services at your university is a scam" carries the full charge with zero profanity. Replace the expletive with a harder *noun*, not a milder adjective.

## Story material

Arka's personal history is the credibility engine, but it is **his to spend, not the team's to mine.** Two tiers:

**Green — use freely, already public:**
- Arrived in Australia at 18, alone, during COVID, with no relatives or support network here.
- Applied to 1,000–2,000 roles with no replies before anything worked.
- Failed two units in first semester and lost a 50% scholarship.
- Worked as a hotel receptionist in Docklands, Melbourne while studying.
- Ended up with 23 job offers before graduating; took a data analyst role at a Melbourne real-estate data company.
- Started Ark Abroad because friends kept asking how he did it.
- First sales experience: running a Diwali mela stall in Delhi as a schoolkid to buy his own phone.

**Amber — check with Arka before every single use:**
Anything involving his health, his family's finances, his relationships, his mental health, or his time running rage-bait content. This material is powerful and he uses it himself, but a team member deploying it in a caption without asking is a different act than the founder choosing to tell it. Ask first, every time. No exceptions, even if he's told the same story publicly before.

## Never ships

These appear in the raw founder transcripts. They do not go into Ark Abroad content in any form, paraphrased or implied.

1. **Any advice to lie or misrepresent in an interview, on a resume, or on LinkedIn.** Arka has described "lying a bit in interviews" as part of a sales mindset. Ark Abroad sells positioning, not fabrication — and clients acting on that advice risk their visa status and any offer they get. Reframe every time as: *tell the truth in the most commercially compelling order.* Framing, emphasis, and translating academic work into business outcomes are all fair. Inventing experience is not.
2. **Generalisations about ethnic, national, or religious communities**, including ones framed as self-criticism of South Asians. Colonial-hangover psychology is a legitimate topic; a claim about how "people from X country think" is not.
3. **Slurs of any kind**, including casual disability language.
4. **Revenge as motivation.** "I'm coming for the people who wronged me" is real fuel for Arka and terrible positioning for a brand selling to people who are already hurting. The brand promise is *get the role you deserve*, not *prove them wrong.*
5. **Claims that racism doesn't exist.** The correct framing is the one Arka actually uses: it exists, and demonstrated value is the lever you control. Never flatten it to "racism isn't real" — it reads as gaslighting to an audience who experiences it.
6. **Named universities, employers, or individuals as villains.** Attack the category — "university career services" — never "[University] career services." Legal and relationship risk with no upside.
7. **Unverified outcome numbers.** See `references/proof-bank.md`. If a figure isn't in there, it doesn't ship.

## Worked rewrites

**Generic → Ark**

> ❌ Many international students struggle to secure roles in the Australian job market. Here are five tips to improve your resume and stand out to employers.

> ✅ You've sent 400 applications. You've heard back from none. It isn't your degree — I had the same degree and got the same silence. It's that your resume is written for a human who doesn't read it and a system that can't parse it. Here's what actually changed for me.

**Motivational fluff → Ark**

> ❌ Believe in yourself! Your dream role in Australia is closer than you think. Keep pushing and never give up on your journey.

> ✅ The people who got the roles you want are not smarter than you. I've worked next to them. They learned to position themselves and you didn't, because nobody taught you. That's a skill gap, not a talent gap. Skill gaps close.

**Corporate → Ark**

> ❌ We're thrilled to announce that Ark Abroad has helped over 100 students secure meaningful employment opportunities across Australia.

> ✅ 100 international students placed. $70,000 to $160,000. Every one of them was told to lower their expectations first.

**Advice with no mechanism → Ark**

> ❌ Networking on LinkedIn is important for your job search. Try to connect with people in your industry.

> ✅ Connection requests don't work. Nobody accepts a stranger with no context. What works is one message that names something specific they built and asks one question a busy person can answer in ten seconds. I sent hundreds. The short ones got replies.

## Before you ship, check

- Does it open cold, with a claim or a story — no preamble?
- Is there a real mechanism, or just encouragement?
- Is there at least one number?
- Is the contempt pointed at a system, never at the reader?
- Could a generic career coach have written any sentence in it? Cut those.
- Is the register right for the channel?
- Anything from **Never ships**?
- Any Amber-tier personal story used without asking Arka?
