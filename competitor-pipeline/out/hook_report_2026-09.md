# Competitor Hook Report — September 2026

The corpus went from 1 tagged hook to 66 this cycle. That's enough to stop reading hooks one at a time and start looking at what the set says.

**What's behind this report:** 46 tracked accounts across AU/US/CA, 1,250 collected posts, 66 outlier videos transcribed and tagged. Every tag below is structural — pattern, angle, narrative shape, brand fit — drafted from the transcript and checked against `reference/arkabroad-voice.md`.

**Read the caveats at the bottom before acting on any of this.** Three of them matter.

---

## 1. The headline: our stated best pattern is our worst performer here

The voice doc says contrarian inversion is *"the highest-performing pattern in the voice. Lead with it when in doubt."* Across 66 competitor outliers, it ranks **sixth of ten**.

| Pattern | Videos | Avg. outlier score |
|---|---|---|
| `warning` | 4 | **37.3x** |
| `direct_question` | 4 | **28.4x** |
| `list` | 13 | 15.2x |
| `cost_accounting` | 13 | 11.3x |
| `bold_statement` | 9 | 7.9x |
| `contrarian_inversion` | 10 | **5.4x** |
| `cold_open_story` | 5 | 5.2x |
| `problem` | 3 | 4.6x |
| `receipt` | 3 | 2.8x |
| `curiosity` | 2 | 2.4x |

Two honest readings of this, and the difference matters:

**The cautious read.** `warning` and `direct_question` have n=4 each. Four videos is not a finding, it's an anecdote with a decimal point. `contrarian_inversion` has n=10, so its 5.4x is the more trustworthy number on this table — and 5.4x above an account's own median is still a strong post.

**The uncomfortable read.** Contrarian inversion is the most *imitated* pattern in this set (10 of 66, second only to `list` and `cost_accounting` at 13 each). Everyone is already doing "you think X, actually Y." It may be underperforming precisely because it's saturated. That doesn't make it wrong for Ark — it makes it not a differentiator on its own.

**What to do with it:** don't drop contrarian inversion. Stop treating it as the reason a video works. Pair it with the two shapes that are actually pulling here — open on a warning or a direct question, *then* invert.

## 2. Length: the middle is winning, not the short end

| Duration | Videos | Avg. outlier score |
|---|---|---|
| ≤60s | 12 | 10.1x |
| 61–150s | 41 | **13.3x** |
| >150s | 13 | 9.0x |

Average outlier length across the whole set is **125 seconds**. The best-performing band is roughly **one to two and a half minutes** — long enough to actually teach a mechanism, short enough not to sprawl.

This cuts against the standard "cut everything under 30 seconds" advice. It also fits the voice: subdivision teaching and cost accounting both need room. You cannot split a role into four sub-roles and price each one in 22 seconds.

## 3. Nobody is publishing anything we could publish

Of 66 tagged hooks: **0 scored `brand_fit = yes`.** 60 came back `with_changes`, 6 outright `no`.

That is not a tagging artefact — it's the clearest competitive signal in the report. Across three markets and 46 accounts, not one competitor produced a video Ark could put out as-is. The recurring failures, in order of frequency:

1. **No mechanism.** Encouragement, adjectives, and reframes with nothing the viewer can do on Monday.
2. **No numbers.** The voice doc demands cost accounting; almost nobody counts anything.
3. **Self-promotion mid-teach.** Paid template plugs, "link in bio," affiliate cross-promos.
4. **Throat-clearing.** "Stay tuned," "if you're new here, my name is —." The voice doc bans this outright.

Meanwhile **65 of 66 are structurally transplantable** (18 `yes`, 47 `with_changes`, 1 `no`). The structures are good and freely available. The substance is the moat.

## 4. The hooks worth stealing this cycle

### 135.3x — "What did you do within twenty days?"
**Thomas Cansino** (@thomascansino, CA/T2) · 131s · 567,000 views · `warning` · [watch](https://www.tiktok.com/@thomascansino/video/7672746393106189589)

A resume audit that stops on one line and asks what a recruiter will do with an employment gap. Structure: *warning clip → section audit → single missing element → verdict.*

**Steal:** month-and-year dates only (exact dates invite gap interrogation), single-column for ATS, metrics attached only to results that have them.
**Fix:** he plugs his own paid resume template mid-review and says "maybe I'm biased," which turns teaching into selling. No numbers anywhere.
**The Ark version:** keep the twenty-days beat, invert it — a recruiter is reading for reasons to *reject*, not reasons to hire. Point the contempt at the screening system, never at the candidate. Add one real number: applications sent, or callback rate.

### 55.7x — "What type of person does every business absolutely need to hire?"
**Brooke Taylor** (@brooketaylorcareercoach, US/T2) · 23s · 248,500 views · `direct_question` · [watch](https://www.tiktok.com/@brooketaylorcareercoach/video/7673223765320355103)

The shortest video in the top ten and the second-highest scorer. *Rhetorical question → unexpected answer → trait list → reframe.*

**Steal:** the shape, exactly as-is. It's clean and it's 23 seconds.
**Fix:** it's pure encouragement — four adjectives with no mechanism for how a hiring manager ever sees or prices those traits. Zero numbers.
**The Ark version:** "every business needs the person who moved countries at 18 alone" — calm in chaos, reads a room fast, manages three stakeholder groups before 9am. But tie each trait to a *named sub-role* (governance analyst vs dashboarding analyst) and the number it produces for an employer, or it lands as flattery.
**Caution:** the source is family-trauma identity content. That's Amber tier. Don't mine a client's story for this.

### 41.0x — "This sounds a little intimidating"
**Caleb Careers** (@calebcareers, US/T2) · 96s · 175,900 views · `cost_accounting` · [watch](https://www.tiktok.com/@calebcareers/video/7670637589438762254)

Takes an intimidating job title and translates it into what the person actually does all day: *"you're not flying the planes or building them, you're using data to keep operations running."*

**This is one step from subdivision teaching** — the exact signature move, arrived at from a different direction. It maps directly onto internationally-trained professionals ruling themselves out of titles they don't recognise.
**Fix:** throat-clearing after the hook, no actionable mechanism beyond a link, and an affiliate cross-promo that reads exactly like the paid-scheme monetisation the brand attacks.
**The Ark version:** title demystified → sub-roles split → the two lines of positioning that make you credible for it → AUD banding, super, work-rights line.

### 58.9x — the one that's unpublishable, and why it's here
**Home From College** (@homefromcollege, AU/T2) · 64s · 110,500 views · `list` · `brand_fit = no`

Scored 58.9x. Trips **Never ships item 4 (revenge as motivation)** — the centrepiece is an admission of writing someone up out of spite, with contempt aimed at a person rather than a system. Also nowhere near register on language.

**One device is still worth taking:** the self-ranking list — *"if you don't listen to a single other piece of advice in this video, please listen to this."* That forces retention on beat one and works for any application-triage or onboarding list.

Everything else is US dorm culture. This account is on the DROP list (see §6).

## 5. What to build next

Three briefs, in priority order, each combining a shape that's performing here with substance competitors don't have:

1. **Warning + resume triage.** Open on the rejection mechanic ("a recruiter is looking for a reason to bin this in six seconds"), audit one section, land on the single missing element. Add the number. ~90–120 seconds.
2. **Direct question + sub-role split.** "What kind of analyst are you actually?" Question → unexpected category → four sub-roles mapped to personality → the salary band each one carries. Uses the highest-scoring shape *and* the signature teaching move.
3. **Title demystification series.** Take the AU corporate titles migrants rule themselves out of and translate each one into what the person does all day, plus the two positioning lines that make you credible. Repeatable format, directly on-mission.

## 6. Roster problem this data exposed

Nine of 25 judged accounts are producing outliers with no usable hook intelligence — job-listing reposters (`Desiree Marie`, `Caleb Careers`, `Career Ghostwriter`, `Kat WFH Hub`) and outright off-topic accounts (travel vlogs, dorm life, comedy skits, security demos).

They still generate outliers because scoring is **relative to each account's own median** — a viral travel clip outranks that account's genuine career content. Nothing upstream filters on what a post is *about*.

Run `npm run roster-quality` for the current list. Pruning them makes every future harvest cheaper *and* every report sharper.

Note the tension: Caleb Careers is flagged DROP for being a listing reposter, and also produced the third-best transplantable hook in this report. Judge the account on its median, not its best post.

## 7. Caveats — read these

1. **`why_it_performed` is empty on all 66 rows.** That field is human-written by standing rule and hasn't been filled in yet. So this report analyses *structure*, not *why any of it landed*. Everything above is a hypothesis about mechanics, not a verified causal claim.
2. **Tags are AI-drafted, not human-verified.** All 64 new rows carry `tagged_by = 'claude-code-draft'`. Spot-check before betting a content calendar on them.
3. **Small samples on the top two patterns.** `warning` and `direct_question` are n=4 each. Treat §1's top rows as a signal to test, not a conclusion.
4. **Outlier score is relative, not absolute.** 135.3x means "unusual for that account," not "high reach." Brooke Taylor's 55.7x post got 248,500 views; Dr. Nonterah's 19.1x got 9,027.
5. **`format` is null on nearly every row.** It's a visual property and transcripts are audio-only, so it was left blank rather than guessed. Talking-head vs screen-walkthrough is still an unanswered question.
6. **`topic_slug` is null on 47 of 66.** The taxonomy has six usable values and the content spans far more. Real subjects are in the free-text `sub_topic` column instead. `/topics` on the dashboard will look sparse until the taxonomy is widened.
7. **T1 contributes nothing.** Nine T1 accounts are harvested but excluded from scoring by design, so they never produce outliers, transcripts, or hooks. They're collection cost with no analytical output right now.
