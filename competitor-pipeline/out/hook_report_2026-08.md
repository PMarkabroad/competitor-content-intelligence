## 1. "Yeah, your life changed completely from a career perspective."

**Who:** Tayo Oloja (@tayooloja) — T2, AU
**Format:** Talking head (two-person interview)
**Pattern:** Cold-open story
**Performance:** 10.1x this account's baseline view-rate (vpf 1.01 vs. baseline 0.10) — the strongest single post we've seen from any tracked competitor so far.

**Watch it:** https://www.tiktok.com/@tayooloja/video/7671922736351989012

### The hook

The video opens mid-conversation, interviewer to guest: *"Yeah, your life changed completely from a career perspective. Tell us where you were before you decided you were gonna change your career."*

The guest doesn't open with the happy ending — she opens with an incident: while working as a youth worker, a young person she was supporting smashed her car up so badly it had to be written off, and she had to get out and run. *"100% life in danger."* That's the cold open. The career-change story only starts after that.

### Why it worked

- **The incident hooks before the topic does.** Nobody scrolls past "my car got destroyed and I ran for my life." By the time the video reveals this is actually a career-advice piece, the viewer is already in.
- **It's a real interview, not a monologue.** A second voice asking questions ("did you feel like your life was in danger?", "tell us what life looks like right now") does a lot of pacing work a single talking head can't — it creates natural beats and stops the piece from feeling like a pitch.
- **The payoff is concrete, if soft.** "I wake up on Saturday and don't have anything to do," "I work from home," "we have capacity to start a business" — these are lifestyle specifics, not vague claims of success.

### Does it transfer to Ark?

**Pattern: yes.** The shape — dramatic, specific incident cold open, interview format, concrete after-state — is culturally portable and lands squarely in Ark's actual audience (a migrant pivoting careers into AU corporate work). Nothing in the transcript is AU-specific in a way that would need re-checking.

**As-is content: needs changes before it's Ark.** Checked against `reference/arkabroad-voice.md`'s "Before you ship" list:

- ✅ Opens cold with a real incident, no preamble.
- ❌ **No real mechanism.** The actual turning point is compressed into one line — *"I found [a program], I reached out, three months later I had an offer"* — with no explanation of what she actually did differently. That's encouragement, not teaching. Ark's voice teaches through subdivision (e.g., what specifically makes a strong business analyst candidate, which sub-role fits which kind of person) — this piece has none of that.
- ❌ **No numbers.** No salary, no application count, no cost, no timeline breakdown beyond "three months." Ark's voice counts everything.
- ❌ **No contrarian inversion.** It's a warm testimonial, not a belief broken by a mechanism.
- ⚠️ **Generic-coach risk.** Once the car-crash cold open ends, most of the rest ("I don't need to be in a rush," "we have dinners," "we can think about starting a business") is the kind of soft lifestyle-upgrade content any recruiter or LinkedIn career coach could have produced.
- ⚠️ It's also a testimonial for a named competitor's own paid program — an Ark version drops that entirely.

**Verdict: `with_changes`.** Keep the structure — a specific, high-stakes incident cold open, interview format, concrete after-state. Replace the testimonial middle with an actual subdivision-taught mechanism and real cost-accounting numbers (comp before/after, applications sent, real timeline), and cut the "here's how I got there" line down to something an Ark clip would actually show, not gesture at.
